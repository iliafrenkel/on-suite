Spring evenings are a good time to start looking up. The nights are still long, the air is often clear, and some of the easiest patterns in the sky are high overhead after dark.

Start with something you can find without a chart. The brightest planet is usually the first point of light to appear in the west after sunset. Once you have it, look for the bright stars around it and learn them one at a time.

Give your eyes twenty minutes away from screens and street lights. Faint stars appear one by one as your eyes adjust, and a sky that looked empty fills up.

Binoculars help more than most people expect. Sweep slowly along the band of the Milky Way and you will see clusters that no naked eye can separate.
