My first sourdough starter died in a week. I fed it when I remembered, kept it somewhere cold, and expected bread by Saturday. The second one lived, mostly because I stopped expecting anything.

A starter is flour, water and time. The flour and water are easy. The time is the hard part: a few days of feeding at the same hour, watching for bubbles, smelling it change from paste to something sour and alive.

What it taught me is patience of a practical kind. Not waiting and hoping, but doing the same small thing every day and trusting it to add up. The bread, when it finally came, was flat and dense and the best thing I had baked.

These days the starter lives in the fridge and gets fed on Sundays. It has outlived two ovens. I still check it for bubbles, out of habit and a little affection.
